The chosen logo, refined

The owner chose this mark and asked us to use it as a base: a sun (or a host's head) above two overlapping green hills, under the LOCAL HOST GEORGIA wordmark. The concept, layout and colours stay the same. The geometry, type and colour are redrawn as precise vectors in all three options. The website uses Option 2.

  1. True geometry on a 12-unit gridThe mark is exactly 12 × 12 units. The sun is a perfect circle of radius 3U filling the top half. Each hill is a true half-ellipse on the base line (5.8U high), so its outer side meets the base vertically, as in the original.
  2. 45° slopes and a right-angled overlapThe straight inner slopes now run at exactly 45° (the original wobbles between 40° and 44°). Each slope is tangent to its dome, so curve and line meet without a kink. The slopes cross at a right angle 4U above the base, which makes the overlap exactly half a square (the "diamond").
  3. Mirror symmetryThe two hills are exact mirror images about the sun's axis. In the original the peaks, the slopes and the sun's centre are each a few pixels off, and the sun is slightly oval. All three options now share this one geometry (the AI drew a slightly different mark in each).
  4. Optical spacingThe sun hovers 0.2U above the ridge line and keeps the same 0.85U gap to both hills (the original's gaps are uneven). The gap stays at least 1 px wide at 16 px.
  5. WordmarkOutfit (geometric, round O, pointed A) matches the original lettering and is outlined, so no font is needed. GPOS kerning plus optical pairs: a wider word space; E–O, R–G and I–A tightened in the letter-spaced GEORGIA. Weights 700, 650 and 500 set the three options. In the horizontal lockup, LOCAL HOST stands on the hills' ridge line.
  6. Gradients and flat versionEvery gradient runs from one light source (top-left, 45°) with three stops. The middle stop is the flat colour, so the flat and gradient versions read the same at small sizes and in print. On dark green the hill greens are lifted in OKLCH lightness so the dark hill does not sink into the background.
  7. Clean filesEach SVG uses true elliptical arcs and a <circle>, has a tight viewBox and gives every gradient an id prefixed with its file name (lh2-horizontal-sun and so on). Internal edges are drawn as an underlay plus planes, so no light seams appear between abutting shapes.
